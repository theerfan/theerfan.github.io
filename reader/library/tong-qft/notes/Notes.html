Notes

Standalone explanations of ideas that underlie quantum field theory, each built up from first principles and readable on its own. Each note is also linked from the passage in the lecture notes where the idea first appears, highlighted there.

So far

  • The Heisenberg inequality. Why a particle confined to a region of size $L$ has $\Delta p \geq \hbar/L$, from Cauchy–Schwarz and $[X, P] = i\hbar$. Linked from the introduction.
  • The Compton wavelength. Where $\hbar/(mc)$ comes from, and why a smaller region is full of particle–antiparticle pairs. Linked from the introduction.
  • The de Broglie wavelength. Why $\lambda_{dB} = h/|\vec p|$ follows from relativity, and how it compares with the Compton length. Linked from the introduction.
  • Relativistic one-particle wave equations. Negative probabilities, the negative-energy tower, and the loss of causality. Linked from the introduction.
  • Spin and statistics. What is experimental input in quantum mechanics, and what relativity forces. Linked from the introduction.
  • Hilbert space and operator-valued fields. What the space is, and what it means to promote a classical coordinate, or a whole field, to an operator. Linked from the introduction.
  • The Planck scale. The 1899 units, and the length, time, mass, and energy they fix. Linked from the introduction.
  • Pointlike electrons, extended protons. What scattering measures, and why spin is not the distinction. Linked from the introduction.
